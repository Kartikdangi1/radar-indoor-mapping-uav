# Radar-Only Indoor Occupancy Mapping for an Unmanned Aerial Vehicle

Smoke, dust and darkness defeat a camera and degrade a LiDAR, precisely the conditions search-and-rescue and industrial-inspection flight most needs reliable mapping under. This repository is a completed Bachelor's thesis project building real-time indoor occupancy mapping for an unmanned aerial vehicle from a single Continental ARS548 4D automotive radar, with no GPS, no camera and no LiDAR-built map anywhere in the pipeline, running end to end on the platform's own Jetson Orin NX compute.

## Background and Motivation

Radar is largely unaffected by the three conditions that defeat camera and LiDAR sensing, and additionally reports Doppler velocity per detection, but it returns a sparse, noisy point cloud by comparison with either alternative, five to twenty points per scan against the hundreds or thousands a LiDAR sweep would return. Every algorithmic choice described below follows from taking that sparsity as a constraint to design around rather than a problem to wait out: a scan-matching algorithm's covariance estimation, an occupancy grid's cell-confidence update, and a multipath-rejection filter's threshold are each tuned specifically for a point cloud two orders of magnitude sparser than the point clouds most existing mapping algorithms were designed against.

## Implementation

### Odometry

Attitude comes from a Madgwick filter integrating gyroscope readings and correcting them against the accelerometer only when the accelerometer's own reading falls within a plausible magnitude range for genuine gravity rather than for gravity confounded with acceleration, since accepting an accelerometer correction during aggressive manoeuvring would inject exactly the acceleration-induced error the correction is meant to remove. Linear velocity comes from the radar's own Doppler measurements rather than from integrating acceleration, resolved by random sample consensus against outlier detections rather than averaged naively, since averaging a sparse detection set containing even one bad point would corrupt the whole velocity estimate rather than being diluted by the other points the way it would be in a denser cloud. Height comes from a downward-facing LiDAR rather than from either the radar or barometric estimation, fused through the same pipeline as a third independent source rather than treated as a fallback. The three sources are combined through an explicit rotation-vector formulation on the group of rigid-body rotations rather than through a simpler but less numerically stable Euler-angle integration, and scan-matching against accumulated submaps using a generalised iterative closest point algorithm, tuned with a small five-point neighbourhood for covariance estimation to suit the radar's sparse returns rather than the larger neighbourhoods a denser point cloud would support, corrects the drift that dead-reckoning integration alone would accumulate unbounded.

### Mapping

A temporal, log-odds occupancy grid accumulates radar detections into a persistent map, its origin driven by the fused odometry described above, compensating for the vehicle's own motion between the start and end of a single scan rather than treating a scan as instantaneous. Occupied cells decay toward the unknown prior over a four-second time constant rather than persisting indefinitely once observed, which lets a transient multipath artefact fade from the map over time while a repeatedly observed real surface, reinforced by every new scan that confirms it, remains above the occupied threshold throughout. A dedicated occlusion filter removes detections that fall behind an already-confirmed surface along the same ray, since a radar return from behind a wall the map has already established is overwhelmingly more likely to be a multipath reflection than a genuine detection of something beyond it. Two separate preprocessing configurations feed the odometry and mapping stages from the same raw scan rather than sharing one configuration between them: a narrower field of view and a more permissive detection-strength threshold favour the odometry path, which benefits from more Doppler-observable points even at low confidence, while a wider field of view and a stricter threshold favour the mapping path, which needs high-confidence detections of genuine static structure rather than the maximum possible point count.

## Validation

An eight-category integration test suite, running in roughly forty seconds, checks node health, topic publication rates against stated minimums for odometry, radar and map updates, the completeness of the transform tree from the map frame down to the radar frame, internal SLAM health, odometry sanity, no undefined values, a properly normalised orientation quaternion, a bounded velocity estimate, a populated covariance, radar point-cloud quality, and occupancy-map quality, a stable cell resolution and genuine growth in both occupied and free space over time, and cross-sensor timestamp synchronisation, and generates a report suitable for direct inclusion in the thesis document itself rather than requiring a separate manual write-up of what was checked.

At approximately 150 milliseconds of end-to-end latency on the Jetson Orin NX, with fused odometry running at 50 hertz, the pipeline reaches a sub-2 per cent endpoint displacement ratio over a 71-metre trajectory and rejects more than 95 per cent of multipath-induced ghost detections, all within under 22 per cent of the platform's available compute. The occupancy grid resolves structure at a 7-centimetre cell size, a resolution chosen to represent room-scale features without demanding a computational or memory budget the sparse radar input could not usefully fill at a finer scale.

## Limitations

No ground-truth trajectory reference was available during data collection, so the endpoint displacement ratio reported above is a self-consistency bound, the distance between the trajectory's start and end divided by its total length, rather than a per-pose accuracy figure such as absolute trajectory error, which cannot be computed without an external reference the recording did not have. This is stated as an inherent constraint of the evaluation setup, not a modelling choice that a different algorithm would have avoided, and any accuracy claim drawn from this thesis should be read with that constraint in mind rather than mistaken for a per-pose error measurement.

## Further Work

The dual-path preprocessing, occlusion filtering and temporal decay developed here for a hand-carried evaluation platform are the same techniques the sibling `drone-radar-camera-fusion` project carries forward onto an actual flying platform with an added camera modality, and validating this thesis's algorithms under real flight dynamics, rather than the hand-carried motion profile used here, is the natural extension already under way in that later project rather than a separate open item in this one.
